What this actually is

Kanji are not arbitrary. Almost every character decomposes into components that recur across thousands of others, and skilled readers exploit that structure constantly. Most software for learning kanji ignores it and drills characters as flat items.

This programme asks what happens when a game is built around that structure instead — and, just as importantly, whether it actually helps anyone learn. That means prototypes on one side and studies with real learners on the other, which is why it needs people from two fields that rarely share a room.

It runs at the Chair of N-Dimensional User Experience (Prof. Johanna Pirker) at TU Munich, with guided research supervised by Dr. David Plecher. A work-in-progress paper is being presented at GALA 2026.
